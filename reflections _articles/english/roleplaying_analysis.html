Roleplaying - Analysis based on my experience

Written on: 11.12.2025

Last revised on: 24.01.2026

I assumed that I would never return to my roleplaying addiction. Unfortunately, reality proved me wrong. The harder I tried to control my compulsion, the more I succumbed to it. The exhaustion of restraining this impulse is what prompted me to conduct an analysis on roleplaying.

I initially regarded it as a narrative-driven video game: provide your character's reaction, give additional instructions to the AI on how to continue, let it answer and repeat. It was unremarkable on the surface. However, it took me a long time to realize that roleplaying actually possesses some detrimental characteristics which make it fundamentally different from conventional video games.

The key difference lies within the nature of the activity itself: roleplaying demands assuming a persona while gaming requires players to pursue objectives within a predetermined set of rules. Therefore, engaging in roleplaying is analogous to daydreaming in an alternate reality that bends to the will of the mind.

Not only that, but roleplaying takes place in an open-ended world, which means the narrative is limited only by the imagination, will and experience of the user. Unlike the real world and games which are strictly regulated, the medium lacks inherent safeguards. Because there is no limit to what themes can be explored within a roleplay, users might externalize their internal states by pursuing inappropriate scenarios such as suicide attempts, abuse and sexual violation. This leaves them at risk of developing or exacerbating mental health struggles.

Additionally, roleplaying with an AI that allows answer regeneration exposes users to a known addictive mechanism, namely "the Slot Machine Effect". What makes this behavior loop even more potent is the lack of material costs and the inherent drive of the users to obtain an ideal answer. Functioning as story continuations, AI-generated answers compel the user to seek the most engaging option to keep their narrative from stagnating.

For someone who is discontented with themselves, roleplaying satisfies their need to escape from their identity and reality. The major problem with this is that it creates a dangerous self-reinforcing loop: the user escapes from their problems into the fictional world of their roleplay, neglecting their real life, therefore the problems that urged them to escape fester, which further compels the user to seek a reprieve in their imaginary reality. What makes this even more concerning is that, because the user assumes a persona for extended periods of time, their identity erodes over time, blurring with that of their character. Additionally, because they succumb to their impulses, their ability to self-control, self-regulate their internal world and to stay in the present atrophies. In other words, excessive roleplaying causes erosion of quality of life, erosion of self and erosion of self-regulating processes.
